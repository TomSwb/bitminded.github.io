About me

My story

I'm Thomas, from the canton of Vaud. My first career was hands-on: a federal certificate as a truck driver, then four years as a full-time volunteer in Japan, Australia and Papua New Guinea, where I ran the galley of a medical ship and kept the accounts for more than 150 volunteers.

Back in Switzerland I joined Medair, an international humanitarian NGO, in recruitment, where I screened around 400 applications a week. There too I ended up building the tools: Excel and Salesforce setups that cut administrative errors by 30 %.

That's when I realised building the tools was the part I loved. In 2024 I started learning to code on my own, and in 2026 I joined 42 Lausanne to do it properly.

Why I build

What I love is taking something from nothing to working, then fixing it and making it better. The stack doesn't matter much: so far it's been a desktop app in C, this website, and two games. What matters is that it works for the people using it. Years of serving people taught me that most of them aren't technical, and that good tools quietly make their day easier. That's what I want to build.

What I bring from before

  • Rigour, and documentation people can actually follow.
  • Experience with large volumes of sensitive personal data, handled carefully.
  • Process thinking: Lean training, and tools that measurably reduced errors.
  • Teamwork across cultures, in English and French, including leading teams of 2 to 15 people.

How I use AI

My journey started in 2024 with AI doing almost everything. I built a whole CRM and several other tools that way, including the first version of this site. Then I reached the point of adding payments and selling to real people, and I stopped: I could not guarantee the safety of people's money and data in code I did not understand.

So I started again. Since 2026 I write all my code by hand. I use AI the way I'd use a patient teacher or a very good search engine: to explain concepts, errors and documentation. It does not write my code. It does help with text: it proofreads my English and French, and it drafts documentation and page text that I then review and correct. Each project's README says exactly how AI was used.

Why "Bitminded"

Bitminded is the name I build under: a bit of code, a mind for people. One day I'd like it to become a small studio making thoughtful, safe software; for now it's the home of my projects.
